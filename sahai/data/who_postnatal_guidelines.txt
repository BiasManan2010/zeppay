WHO Postnatal Care Guidelines — Danger Signs (excerpt for SahAI demo RAG)

Postnatal danger signs in the mother require immediate referral or emergency care.

SEVERE BLEEDING: Soaking more than one pad in an hour, or bleeding increases rather than decreases after the first day, is a danger sign. The mother may feel dizzy, weak, or faint. Refer immediately to a facility capable of managing obstetric emergencies.

FEVER: A temperature above 38°C (100.4°F) in the postnatal period may indicate infection such as endometritis or wound infection. Ask about foul-smelling discharge, abdominal pain, and chills. Urgent assessment is needed.

SEPSIS SIGNS: Fast breathing, confusion, very high or low temperature, and feeling extremely unwell can indicate sepsis. This is life-threatening and requires emergency care.

HEADACHE AND VISUAL DISTURBANCE: Severe headache with blurred vision or spots may suggest postpartum hypertension or eclampsia. Check blood pressure. Refer urgently if systolic BP is 140 or higher with symptoms.

BREATHING DIFFICULTY: Shortness of breath at rest, chest pain, or inability to speak in full sentences is a danger sign. May indicate pulmonary embolism, pneumonia, or cardiac complications.

BREAST PROBLEMS: Red, hot, painful breast with fever may indicate mastitis. Engorgement alone is common but severe pain with fever needs treatment.

PERINEAL OR CESAREAN WOUND: Increasing pain, redness, swelling, or pus from episiotomy or cesarean wound suggests infection. Refer for assessment.

EMOTIONAL WELLBEING: Persistent sadness, inability to care for self or baby, thoughts of harming self or baby require psychosocial support and referral. Postpartum depression is common and treatable.

NEWBORN DANGER SIGNS (mother should report): Not feeding, convulsions, fast breathing, chest in-drawing, fever or hypothermia, umbilical redness, yellow skin, or lethargy.

COUNSELING: Health workers should ask about danger signs at every postnatal visit. Teach families to recognize these signs and seek care without delay. Document vitals including blood pressure, temperature, and pulse at each contact.

REFERRAL: When any danger sign is present, do not delay referral while waiting for laboratory results unless the facility can manage the condition locally. Accompany the mother when possible and communicate findings to receiving staff.

LOW-RISK FOLLOW-UP: Mothers without danger signs should be counseled on nutrition, breastfeeding, family planning, and when to return for routine postnatal checks within the first six weeks.

MID-RISK MONITORING: Borderline vitals or mild symptoms warrant closer follow-up within 24–48 hours, repeat vitals, and escalation if symptoms worsen.

HIGH-RISK: Combination of abnormal vitals (elevated blood pressure, tachycardia, hyperglycemia) with any reported symptom should trigger immediate supervisor notification and referral preparation.
